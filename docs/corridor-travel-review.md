# Safe corridor travel — #286 and #287

## #286: observable eligibility and stop policy

`CorridorTravel.ts` projects the same visible/remembered map information as the UI before making a pure route decision. It never consults hidden room metadata, unseen terrain, hidden actors, future random rolls or an explored-floor pathfinder. With fog disabled, the policy sees the full map just as the player does. Loot is visible-only; static objects/doors and landmarks follow map memory. Player health, poison and visible damage history are observable inputs.

An explicit direction may start only on an otherwise quiet, known corridor. Each next tile and its cardinal surroundings must be known. A route stops before doors/objects, loot, stairs/the entrance, junctions and room mouths. A known 2×2 open patch is treated as a room, without consulting the generated room list. A sole onward path may bend; a dead end stops without a blocked action. Step off a landmark manually before starting. Any visible living creature blocks running, including idle or controlled creatures; this deliberately favors safety over guessing intent.

After an ordinary step, stop for terminal state, floor change, damage, poison, a visible creature, newly visible objects/loot/landmarks or newly revealed room/junction geometry. Damage history catches a hit even if same-turn healing restores the net health. Existing poison denies starting without spending a turn. The policy returns a reason or one direction; it does not move, consume, fight, interact, pick up, transition floors or spend turns.

Readable tests cover known routes/bends, unknown boundaries, junctions/rooms, ordinary and elite doors, traps, gold, chests, merchants, fountains, loot, landmarks, poison, hidden-information invariance, damage and ordinary per-step turn/mana equivalence. Controller/UI work belongs to #287; policy alone does not expose a faster-movement command. Saves and generation are unchanged.

## #287: paced command and cancellation

Press **R**, then **Arrow/WASD** to choose the initial direction. Selection and denied starts are free; **Escape** cancels selection or running. A known unambiguous bend follows its sole onward tile. Each allowed step is scheduled after 120ms and calls the existing Home/session `move` action: ordinary turns, enemy phases, effects, mana recovery and discovery remain owned by the existing resolver. There is no loop resolving a whole corridor in one render and no new action/save contract. `CORRIDOR_STEP_MS` changes presentation pace only.

The controller is transient React state/ref ownership. The ref invalidates a queued callback synchronously when canceled; effect cleanup clears its timer, and callbacks also check map focus and document visibility. Ordinary manual gameplay keys cancel before their existing handler, including the earlier spell/Character branches. Pointer input, focus leaving the map, window blur, hidden document, modal/input gating and terminal/lifecycle changes stop travel. Closing a menu or restoring an adventure never resumes it. Key repeat cannot arm or duplicate a run. A denied or unexpectedly uncommitted step stops rather than rescheduling indefinitely.

The visible status cue reports direction selection, running and the stop reason without adding activity events or saved instructions. Help documents the command and conservative limits. Map projection is computed only while running, avoiding an additional full-grid projection during ordinary manual play.

Controller tests exercise paced steps, free selection/cancel/denial, repeat protection, manual movement and other gameplay-key interruption, pointer/focus/blur/visibility changes and input disable/re-enable. Production browser flows exercise actual movement, automatic room stop, free cancellation, menus, restored saves, focus and fog-enabled discovery. The existing save/terminal/browser suite is retained. The broader seed/lifecycle/performance matrix in #288 remains a separate backlog story; human usability acceptance is still required. The conservative geometric room rule can stop early in widened corridors, and any visible living creature blocks running even if currently idle or controlled.
